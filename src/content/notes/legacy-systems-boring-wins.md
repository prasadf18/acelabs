title: "Digital transformation is mostly boring, and that's the point"
description: "Why replacing a spreadsheet-driven process usually beats adding AI on top of it."

When a company says it wants to "transform digitally," the project that follows usually involves a chatbot. The real problem, more often than not, is a spreadsheet that three people edit on Fridays.

That spreadsheet is a system. It has inputs, rules nobody wrote down, and one person who knows why column K is hidden. Adding AI on top doesn't fix it. It just makes the confusion faster.

## Start by drawing what exists

Before writing any code, map the process as it really runs: who touches the data, where it gets copied by hand, and what breaks when someone is on leave. This usually takes a few days and produces a page of boxes and arrows. It is the most valuable page in the project.

## Replace first, then add intelligence

Most wins come from plain engineering: a database instead of a shared file, a scheduled job instead of a reminder, a form with validation instead of free-text email. Once the process is reliable, AI has something solid to work with, such as extracting fields from documents, drafting replies, or flagging odd records.

## Measure the boring numbers

Track time per task, error rate, and how often someone has to step in manually. If those numbers don't move, the project isn't working, however impressive the demo looks.

Good transformation work is quiet. The Friday spreadsheet disappears, and nobody notices except the people who used to maintain it.
